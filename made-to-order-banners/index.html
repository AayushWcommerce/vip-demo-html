Where it goes

  • Home page, after the two "Shop by craft" rows, before "Bought & loved". Today the Made to Order row sits 3rd, right after Bundle offers.
  • By then the shopper has seen everything in stock: Newly arrived, Bundles, Occasions, Under ₹2,000, Pinboard, Categories, Crafts.
  • The craft rows prime "handmade, heritage", so a weaver story lands best right after them.
  • One banner, not a product row. Tap opens /made-to-order (already built, with filters).
  • Not in saree/category listings or search filters as cards (already hidden there).

Why this protects normal sales

  • In-stock pieces get the first screens; made-to-order is an extra option for shoppers who kept scrolling (higher intent, browsing for something special).
  • A banner asks for one tap into a separate page, so a made-to-order piece never sits next to a "ships tomorrow" saree in the same grid.
  • Pre-order, prepaid and the wait are said up front, so nobody is surprised at checkout.

How others handle it

  • Marketplaces (e.g. Etsy) label made-to-order items and show the making time before purchase.
  • Indian handloom and craft brands usually keep made-to-order / pre-order in a separate collection with its lead time, promoted with a banner or campaign, not mixed into in-stock grids.
  • Common rules: lead time shown everywhere, payment terms clear, the maker's story as the reason to wait.
